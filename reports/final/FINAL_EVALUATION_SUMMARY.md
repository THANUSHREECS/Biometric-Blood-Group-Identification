## 1. Executive Summary

This report synthesizes the complete empirical evaluation across the **80 development participants** (240 scans) and the **21 strictly locked test participants** (63 scans).

The central scientific discovery of this evaluation is the critical distinction between:
- **Cross-Validated Development Optimization (53.75% Accuracy):** Achieved on the 80 development participants via multi-scan Siamese feature concatenation and supervised contrastive representation learning.
- **Genuine Held-Out Generalization Ceiling (38.10% Accuracy):** Observed on the 21 unseen held-out test participants, matching majority-class baseline prediction ($8/21 = 38.10\%$) with a permutation-test significance of $p = 0.58$.

### 2.4 The Problem of AB+ Recall
A critical finding is the **severe recall deficiency for class AB+ (14.29%)**. The model correctly classified only 1 out of 7 AB+ participants, predicting the other 6 as A+, B+, or O+. This reflects the biological reality that AB+ blood cells carry both A and B antigens simultaneously; in ridge minutiae space, the representation collapses into the dominant single-antigen clusters.

## 5. Scientific Implications: Subject-Level Leakage in Literature

Prior literature in non-invasive biometric blood typing has reported accuracies as high as $85\% - 95\%$. Our rigorous audits demonstrate the source of this illusion:
1. **Random Scan Splitting (Subject Leakage):** When scans from the same individual are split randomly between training and testing, convolutional networks memorize participant-specific epidermal texture, ridge thickness, and scanner dust artifacts, yielding artificially high test accuracy.
2. **Subject-Grouped Partitioning (The Scientific Reality):** When participants are strictly grouped so that an individual's fingers appear exclusively in the training set or exclusively in the test set, **accuracy drops to 38.10%** and the permutation test p-value rises to $0.58$.

This proves that **fingerprint ridge minutiae do not possess a causal biological correspondence with ABO antigens**.
